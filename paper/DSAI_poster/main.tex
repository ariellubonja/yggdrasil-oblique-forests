\block{Sparse Oblique Forests and Why They Train Slowly}{
\begin{itemize}
\item Sparse oblique (SPO) splits are a very powerful tree splitter, part of the top-ranked configuration in Google's Yggdrasil Decision Forests (YDF). They improve accuracy while keeping interpretability. In large datasets, these methods outperform LLMs, while using vastly less energy and resources.
\item However, they are significantly more expensive than traditional tree splitters. Projections are sampled at runtime, so classic optimizations such as presorting cannot be used:
      see figure.
\item \textbf{We improve this runtime by up to 10$\times$}, with our novel approaches adding up to $2\times$ on top of our highly optimized fork of YDF, where we achieved $5\times$ speedup over the original library. We contribute this work to the open-source community as well as submitting to SPAA-27.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item Contrary to prevalent understanding, \textbf{tree training time is not proportional to depth}. Very large nodes near the root cost due to the $O(n\log n)$ cost of sorting, but many small nodes at mid-depth also mount up: small per-node overhead compounds as n. nodes grows exponentially until about $\frac{1}{3}$ down the tree.
\item GBTs train to depth~6; RFs to depth 16--24 or to purity. The two families stress opposite ends of the
      depth range, so we contribute one optimization for each.
\end{itemize}
}

\block{1. Vectorized Histogram Filling (AVX2 / AVX-512)}{
\begin{itemize}
\item Histogramming costs $O(n\log k)$ but has high startup cost, whereas sort costs $O(n\log n)$ but little startup. We target histograms, as they work best in the GBT (shallow) tree regime.
\item We propose hierarchical vectorized histogram filling: a \textbf{two-pass vector compare instead of a binary search, reducing histogram cost to $O(n)$}. This leverages AVX2 and AVX-512 CPU vector instructions. 
\end{itemize}

\block{2. Runtime-Dynamic Histograms}{
\begin{itemize}
\item In deep trees, the number of active nodes grows exponentially and peaks about halfway to purity.
      These nodes hold few samples, yet histograms still pay the $O(k)$ bin generation. Our vectorized approach costs $O(n) + O(k)$.
\item Dynamic switching picks vectorized histograms or exact splits based on the n. samples in the node
      (breakeven $\approx$250 rows for AVX2/64 bins, $\approx$1000 for AVX-512/256 bins).
\end{itemize}

\block{Conclusions}{
\begin{itemize}
\item AVX2 / AVX-512 vectorized histogram filling outperforms the baseline on every dataset: 1.3--1.6$\times$ on depth-6 SPO-GBT, up to 2$\times$ on SPO-RF.
\item Dynamic switching by node size: 1.2--1.6$\times$ from depth 16 to purity, with no detectable accuracy change.
\item Together they bring SPO-RF training cost on par with YDF's traditional axis-aligned RF, while preserving the accuracy advantage of SPO.
\item Fixes contributed upstream to YDF as a series of pull requests.
\end{itemize}
}
